# v4 deadline, credit, memory and exploration experiments

Status: **complete**.

Main training: **356,515,840 transitions across 55 independent runs**, plus 294,912 calibration transitions. Five paired training seeds. Unchanged v4 gameplay, primitive actions, legal masks, no demonstrations or prerequisite shaping.

Validation worlds: 8,000,001–8,000,128; one-shot reserved test: 9,000,001–9,000,128. Sampler 54321. The test split is now consumed.

## What improved, and what did not

The deadline correction is an objective-correctness fix, not a demonstrated cure
for progression. Its five paired 4M differences average +0.98/32 for MLP and
+1.37/32 for targets, but most of either mean comes from one seed. Exploratory
paired Student-t 95% intervals span zero (MLP −1.98 to +3.94; targets −2.93 to
+5.66). Five seeds do not establish a reliable performance gain.

Memory produced the strongest **early reliability** evidence: 4M final scores
14.80 ± 0.40/32 and 99.1% stone collection, versus the finite-target control's
14.04 ± 2.96 and 79.5%. However, memory won only two of five paired score
comparisons; its small mean advantage is not a robust score improvement. At
16M both methods collected stone in every evaluation world and averaged
17.95 versus 17.79/32. The paired memory-minus-control differences were
[+0.055, +0.961, +1.453, −1.344, −0.313]; mean +0.163, sample SD 1.097.
Memory's mean death rate was also higher: 17.2% versus 12.5% at 16M.
Recurrence, extra capacity and sequence optimization are bundled here, so this
does not isolate memory as the causal mechanism.

None of the other 4M alternatives beat the finite-target control's mean:
undiscounted 11.39, longer trace 12.54, longer rollout 13.49, and RND 11.60/32.
These are negative results for these configurations/budgets, not evidence that
generic exploration or long-horizon learning cannot work. Only memory qualified
for the predeclared 16M alternative; RND has no matched 16M result in this study.

**The progression bottleneck remains furnace setup, not acquiring stone.** At
16M the control made a furnace in 7/640 evaluation episodes; memory in 4/640,
with one anvil. The selected memory policy made none on its 128-world test,
despite collecting stone in all worlds. Its separate validation diagnostic
trajectories accumulated peak stone of 241 on average, yet furnace crafting was
legal on **zero actions across all 128 episodes**. Peak wood averaged only
13.45, with median 13, while a workbench costs 10 and a furnace needs another
4 wood, 20 stone, 3 torches and a nearby placed workbench. Those inventory peaks
are marginal, not simultaneous prerequisites: they cannot establish which
condition was missing on each action. Resource retention, station placement and
returning to a station are the next hypotheses to separate with event telemetry.
Downstream smelting/tool recipes never became legal in those diagnostics.

The selected test episodes averaged **1513 actions after their last new
achievement**. The learner has discovered a useful mining/exploration/combat
policy, but not a reliable transition into sustained crafting. Adding memory or
this RND bonus did not resolve that transition. Previous ore-supply/tool-ownership
limits still constrain eventual saturation, but cannot explain this early gate.

Memory cost about **1.41× mean measured wall/run** at 16M: 51.4 versus 36.4
minutes including validation. Optimization accounted for 79.6% of its recorded
collection-plus-optimization time, versus 75.6% for the control; collection also
includes policy inference. Timings are contended CPU measurements, not isolated
equal-FLOP comparisons, and the coarse equal-wall snapshots do not establish a
memory efficiency win. The simulator is still not the dominant measured phase.

The selected 18.83/32 test is not a new best over the earlier study's preserved
20.79/32 policy. They used different fresh test worlds, so the difference is not
a paired regression estimate. Keep both artifacts and the current defaults.
Next: prerequisite-level telemetry, isolated v5 simulator timing, and fresh
matched v4/v5 learner trials before any game or model promotion. No such trial
was started by this completion follow-up.

## Deadline correction

The C API labels the action budget as a truncation. For our inherent 3000-action objective it has zero future reward, so both feed-forward and recurrent learners now stop value bootstrap there by default. The explicit legacy control preserves the old behavior for this comparison. The simulator itself is unchanged. Unfinished rollout fragments still bootstrap normally.

Regression tests check exact final-action targets, death/deadline overlap, no reset-world leakage, finite undiscounted Monte Carlo returns and nonterminal fragment bootstrap. Historical mechanics/parity tests did not establish objective correctness.

`mlp` finite-minus-legacy final score: mean **+0.980**, paired seed differences [0.46875, 0.046875, 5.171875, -0.078125, -0.7109375]. Correct objective semantics do not automatically imply a large performance improvement.

`targets` finite-minus-legacy final score: mean **+1.366**, paired seed differences [-1.09375, 0.5625, -0.71875, 7.3984375, 0.6796875]. Correct objective semantics do not automatically imply a large performance improvement.

## Limits and interpretation

- Undiscounted reward, longer GAE trace, longer fragments, recurrent memory and novelty are separate screen conditions relative to finite_targets. Longer fragments also change batch/refresh cadence; recurrence adds capacity and sequence batching. Do not attribute either package solely to one mechanism.
- RND uses generic observation prediction error, not a recipe/goal controller. It is training-only, capped at .005/action, and persistent; benchmark evaluation contains only achievement/death rewards. This condition is not an extrinsic-only training baseline.
- The alternative was selected by five-seed FINAL 4M means, then both it and finite_targets restarted at 16M. Cross-budget learning-rate schedules differ. Screen selection is optimistic; the matched long comparison and one-shot test are separate evidence.
- Five training seeds improve reliability estimates but do not prove a universal algorithm ranking. Equal-transition trials have different neural compute costs, and up to three jobs shared an active desktop. JSON includes phase timings and coarse same-wall snapshots, not an exact equal-FLOP comparison.
- Ore-supply and tool-ownership limitations from the previous study remain. This study does not change the game to raise scores or claim all achievements are jointly feasible.
